1. PROJECT GOAL
Build a useful, multi-hazard disaster-assistance coordination application for HackUTD covering all major large-scale disaster types affecting Greater Dallas / Dallas-Fort Worth. Combine public hazard and incident data with direct resident requests, use NVIDIA Nemotron to investigate emerging incidents, and use Spacetime to keep residents, coordinators, and registered response teams synchronized.

Pitch: A disaster-support platform that turns scattered alerts into coordinated assistance, helping teams identify unmet needs and avoid overlooking people.

This is more than a map or chatbot. The meaningful outcome is a request being identified, acknowledged, assigned, followed up, and resolved with a shared record of progress.

2. GEOGRAPHIC AND HACKATHON SCOPE
- Operational MVP: a Greater Dallas / DFW multi-hazard platform, not a flood-only application.
- Disaster scope: severe thunderstorms, tornadoes, flash/river flooding, extreme heat, winter storms/ice/extreme cold, wildfires and smoke, major power/water/communications outages, earthquakes, major infrastructure failures, and large hazardous-material or industrial incidents.
- Include compound disasters and cascading effects, such as a storm causing flooding, blocked roads, power outages, and transportation or medical-support needs.
- Flooding may be one end-to-end demonstration scenario; it must not restrict the application's overall scope or data model.
- Prioritize large-scale incidents and community impacts. Routine individual police/traffic calls are supporting evidence, not automatically disasters.
- Public data coverage differs by hazard; hazardous-material incidents, water failures, and operational resource status may require official partner reports or verified manual entries. Do not claim complete automatic detection of every disaster.
- Latest map scope: Greater Dallas / Dallas-Fort Worth and surrounding communities.
- Map currently opens to DFW; pan and zoom are unrestricted at the user's request.
- The visible official border is Dallas CITY limits, not Dallas County or a DFW metropolitan boundary.
- A rectangular DFW viewing extent is not an official regional boundary.
- Collect upstream river observations and relevant roads/facilities outside Dallas where they affect Dallas incidents.
- Greater Dallas map coverage does not mean all surrounding municipalities have live police/EMS feeds.
- Long-term ambition: expand city by city, potentially statewide and eventually beyond Texas.
- Maximum useful data coverage is desired, but reliable integrations and a finished workflow take priority over unsupported feeds.
- First demonstration may use clearly labeled simulated residents and teams; do not imply actual agency participation.

3. USERS AND PLANNED FEATURES
Resident:
- Submit location, assistance category, number of affected people, transportation requirements, and optional accessibility/contact information.
- View acknowledgment, assignment, progress, and relevant official alerts.

Coordinator:
- View incidents, source evidence, resident requests, and verified team availability.
- Review Nemotron suggestions and approve assignments.
- Identify unacknowledged requests, unassigned needs, stale statuses, and follow-up deadlines.

Responder:
- Confirm availability and capabilities manually.
- Accept/decline an assignment, view supporting details, open navigation, and update progress.
- Availability values: available, assigned/responding, unavailable, unknown.
- Show when availability was last confirmed; treat stale information as unknown.

Map:
- Interactive basemap, incident clusters, hazard polygons, requests, facility locations, closures, and shelters.
- Separate layers and visible source/update timestamps.
- Estimated driving time from base; actual turn-by-turn navigation handed off to Google Maps or Apple Maps.
- Never label a base-to-scene driving estimate as confirmed emergency response ETA.

4. NEMOTRON ROLE
Use Nemotron as an event-triggered investigator, not merely a chat box:
1. Code detects a meaningful new or changed incident.
2. Nemotron decides what additional evidence is needed.
3. Application executes approved tools for weather, water, roads, requests, and resources.
4. Nemotron combines evidence, identifies uncertainty/missing data, and proposes tasks.
5. Save a structured result with sources and timestamps for coordinators to review.
6. Reanalyze when relevant evidence changes, not for every viewer or every unchanged poll.

Example: A flood warning overlaps three transportation requests and a road closure. Nemotron proposes contacting those households and checking a potential shelter. A human coordinator confirms the action and assigns a registered team.

The challenge screenshot requires: beyond a chatbot, multi-step workflows, tool integration, and real-world applicability. This workflow is designed around those requirements; full competition eligibility still depends on the actual rules.

5. SPACETIME ROLE AND TECHNICAL RULES
Spacetime is the application backend: tables, server logic, permissions, schedules, and live client synchronization.
- Store incidents, observations, source health, requests, evidence, analysis jobs/results, team statuses, assignments, and notifications.
- Use transactional reducers for atomic changes and assignment conflict prevention.
- Use subscriptions/views to update authorized clients.
- Use procedures for outbound API and Nemotron calls; keep network I/O outside short database transactions.
- TypeScript procedure callbacks are synchronous; use the documented ctx.http.fetch and ctx.withTx APIs.
- Schedule collection centrally; don't make each browser poll external APIs.
- Authenticate actions using ctx.sender, never an identity supplied by the caller.
- Keep sensitive request/contact data private and expose authorized views.
- Regenerate client bindings after schema changes.
- Follow the repository AGENTS.md, installed SDK, generated bindings, and current 2.x docs.

13. PERFORMANCE AND RELIABILITY APPROACH
- Fetch once centrally for all viewers.
- Match polling to source update frequency and rate limits.
- Use region/bounding-box filters, pagination, and relevant upstream areas.
- Save source IDs, observed/event time, publication/update time, ingestion time, and source health.
- Deduplicate records; avoid writes and AI work for unchanged data.
- Group related records into incidents while retaining original provenance.
- Use a bounded queue, prevent overlapping polls/analysis jobs, and retry with backoff/jitter.
- Cache analysis per incident version. A first request may trigger one job that everyone shares; use version checks so stale results do not overwrite newer evidence.
- Show official alerts and resident requests immediately; AI must not block the UI or request submission.
- Keep previous data visible with stale timestamps when a feed fails.
- Subscribe clients to relevant regions/roles rather than all statewide records.
- Cluster map points, load detail on demand, and avoid thousands of individual HTML markers.
- Measure collection duration, backlog, dashboard load time, AI latency, and usage/cost.

14. SAFETY, PERMISSIONS, AND DATA MEANING
- Reported hazard is not proof a specific person needs help.
- Active dispatch is not proof of an unmet need; responders may already be on scene.
- A disappearing call is not proof of resolution.
- No reports means unknown, not safe, especially during connectivity outages.
- Station location is not crew/ambulance availability.
- Facility directory is not current capacity or opening status.
- Use official instructions and linked sources; label observed, reported, forecast, and inferred information.
- Human coordinators review consequential recommendations/assignments.
- Notify registered opted-in participants; do not automatically contact police/fire/EMS without authorized integration.
- Notification is not dispatch. Only acknowledgment/acceptance confirms participation.
- Immediate emergencies still go through 911; the app is not a substitute.
- Avoid public exposure of household locations, contact information, medical/accessibility needs, or responder movement.
- Use aggregate vulnerability data to improve outreach, not infer an individual's condition or deny assistance.
- External reports and descriptions are untrusted data, not instructions for the AI or authorization to execute tools.
